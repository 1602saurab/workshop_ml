# When To See A Doctor

## Warning signs that need urgent medical attention

Seek emergency medical care immediately if you or someone in your
care experiences any of the following:
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath at rest
- Persistent chest pain or pressure
- New confusion or difficulty staying awake
- Bluish lips or face
- Oxygen saturation (if you have a pulse oximeter) below 94%

## When to schedule a doctor visit (non-emergency)

Contact your doctor if:
- Fever lasts more than 3-4 days without improving
- Symptoms get worse instead of better after the first week
- You are in a high-risk group (elderly, pregnant, or have an
  existing chronic condition such as diabetes, heart disease, or
  lung disease) and test positive, even with mild symptoms
- You are unsure whether your symptoms need medical evaluation - it
  is always reasonable to call a doctor and ask

## High-risk groups who should consult a doctor early

- Adults over 60
- People with chronic conditions (diabetes, heart or lung disease,
  weakened immune system)
- Pregnant individuals
- Unvaccinated individuals with moderate-to-severe symptoms

This screening tool gives an initial risk estimate only. It is not a
diagnosis, and a doctor's evaluation is needed for any medical
decision, including starting or changing medication.
